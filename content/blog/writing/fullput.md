## 1 Rami

Rami[^rami] was the Safety And Wellbeing Officer on the mission which in retrospect required 
none, which was a strange thing considering how common workplace accidents were,
moreso if your workplace was a spaceship.

To add on to the mystique, no one knows for sure when the "Wellbeing" part was tagged on.
Or maybe the "Safety" thing came after, actually. In fact, he's pretty sure this 
wasn't his original title. He was Officer of something, that's for sure. In any 
large-enough organisation, everyone is an officer of something.

He thinks maybe it was changed to be something more obviously unambiguous. Before
that, he had been asked countless times by his crewmates, "What is it you do again?"
Between that and the 11-lightyear journey, they couldn't get to their destination any sooner.

And there was another thing he wasn't sure about. The crew members liked to use 
the phrase "Full Put"[^fullput] in just about any context he could recall.

The nature of the job was touted to be quite a dangerous mission, to retrieve some 
thing or another of cosmic importance[^macguffin], not to mention the already-present 
danger in navigating space itself.

Still, his crewmates were pretty relaxed about it. Gareth wanted to bring on Coca-Cola 
as a provision, but apparently they didn't have enough runway to formulate something 
that could last for the voyage. Miraculously, they did. Well, in Gareth's own words,
he'd try to *Full Put* the process. Whatever that meant. 

The technology was just as strange as the vocabulary. Gareth claimed to have come 
back from the future, after months of R&D, with the formula for powdered Coke. Well,
the other kind. Just like that, the RSSS[^rsss] Maxwell was stocked with Coca-Cola.

## 2 Safety

Rami served a very important role, something more important than Coke Procurement, 
it seemed. He had a specific seat in the cockpit, which he supposes was meant to 
give him a view of everyone that had been defrosted from cryosleep and working the active shift.

The warm bodies of the RSSS Maxwell all fit in the cockpit, and anything that 
didn't immediastely require human attention was in attendance of robots scurrying 
elsewhere on the ship.

Cherie, the Manifest Officer, was in charge of making sure everything was accounted for.
All the moving parts, living or not living, on the ship needed to stay put. Her seat 
was not too far from Rami, and it was the only one that didn't swivel, or maybe she just 
never tried to spin it.

She would always glance at Rami, who would always glance at everyone else. Maybe 
she felt that he would stay put less often than the rest of the crew, but that's a 
totally unfounded belief.

## 3 Manifest

The manifest slug made a straight line from Cherie's barrel to Rami's torso. Another clean 
shot. Its viscous fluid made its slow passage through Rami's veins. Cherie's gun,
a modified tranquiliser gun, seemed to extend at the barrel;
in fact, the entire gun seemed to melt out of her arms in an instant.

In those moments, it tried to occupy two places at the same time, 
oscillating between the two spots so rapidly it seemed to stretch,
until one gun settled and stilled clearly, cleanly, like a jigsaw piece.

Every other being, object, body, warped in the same way... as the fluid evaporated
in Rami's now-temporarily-unconscious body, space negotiated its place with him
as the centre of an incredible whirlpool of liquid flesh, metal, plasma, dust.

The squall settled in almost the same instant. Rami saw nothing, didn't even register 
that he had been out cold for a microsecond. The machinery seemed to go quiet for a moment 
before emitting the same loud hum.

"..ull Put this... " echoed Ahmed's voice from the front of the cockpit before 
it was drowned out by fans and plasma, then some sighs from the crew around him.

Rami wondered what that phrase could mean, but it seemed to bring good luck to the mission.

## Afterword

This story is based on a dream I had on the night of 14 October. 
If I'm lucky enough to wake up lucid enough to recall my dreams, I try to write 
them down on my phone. 

This is an attempt to turn it into writing, and format them into something that makes sense,
adding a bit of information here and there to make it a cohesive story.
I'd been reading Closing Time, the sequel to Catch-22, both books I absolutely adore,
so I believe the writing style may have had an infectious influence on this piece as well.

It's eerie that the way they presented themselves to my dreaming self,
they were already rather well-structured, and I hardly had anything to change.
I wish I could show you what I saw in my mind's eye, but this is the best I can manage.
I tried to keep this story as short as possible.

In my actual dream, it was a lot more dramatic than this. Rami had an emotional 
breakdown, for some reason. He'd figured out the whole space-time reset mechanism
and was upset to the point of throwing up. 

I tried to think of a suitable mechanism for this sort of thing. Why would someone be 
used as the unwitting "reset point"? So, I used the phrase "Full Put" as a sort of 
*activation phrase*, like a way for others to *save* their point in time and space.

Maybe the chemicals in the manifest slug are able to access the deep subconscious
impressions of Rami's (or any subject's) memories, unlocking some potential of the 
brain that's hard for technology to match. With the context of this being a fiction
borne from a dream, I think it's quite apt as an element of the story.

As for why Rami has to be the one, or how reality gets warped in the first place,
well, I can't answer that...
